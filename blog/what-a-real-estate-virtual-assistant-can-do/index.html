What a Real Estate Virtual Assistant Can (and Cannot) Do

By Real Estate Virtual Staff · September 22, 2026

The short version

A real estate virtual assistant can do almost everything in your business that happens on a screen or a phone and does not require a license: lead follow-up, cold calling, transaction coordination, listing input, showing coordination, CRM management, marketing and bookkeeping support. What they cannot do is show property, negotiate on your behalf, give licensed advice or place a sign in a yard.

Tasks a real estate VA handles well

  • Answering new leads by text and email within minutes, then nurturing them in your CRM
  • Cold calling expireds, FSBOs, circle-prospecting and investor lists and booking appointments
  • Opening transaction files, building timelines, routing e-signatures and chasing lenders and title
  • Entering listings into the MLS, writing descriptions, scheduling photos and signs, coordinating showings
  • Collecting showing feedback and sending weekly seller updates
  • Cleaning and tagging your database, building drip campaigns and action plans
  • Designing listing and just-sold graphics, scheduling social posts, editing short videos, sending newsletters
  • Requesting reviews after closings and keeping your Google Business Profile current
  • Tenant communication, maintenance intake and vendor coordination for property managers
  • Research: pulling comps, building prospect lists, summarizing market data

Tasks that stay with the licensed agent

Rules differ by state and brokerage, but the principle is consistent: anything that involves representing a client, negotiating terms, interpreting contracts for a client or being physically present in your market stays with you or a licensed teammate. Your VA prepares, organizes and follows up; you advise, negotiate and show.

How to split the work on day one

Write down everything you did last week and mark each item as screen work or in-person work. The screen work is your VA's job description. Most agents find that between half and two-thirds of their week was screen work, which is why one full-time VA usually frees two or three days.

Start with the repeatable tasks: follow-up, listing admin and transaction paperwork. Add calling and marketing once the basics run on their own. If you are not sure which role to hire first, our team will tell you honestly based on where your time goes.

What to expect from a vetted VA

A properly vetted real estate VA has already done this work for another agent or team, speaks fluent English, knows the common tools, and works your hours.
